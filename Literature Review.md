# Literature Review

## 1. Hasselsteen et al. (2024), “Resource management at modern construction sites: Bridging the gap between scientific knowledge and industry practice and needs.”

📄 Read Full Paper
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301479724018218?

### The authors did two important things:
Systematic literature review — they examined existing research on resource management and resource-consumption reduction at construction sites.
Interviews with construction practitioners — they interviewed 10 industry practitioners to see whether the solutions proposed by researchers actually work in real construction environments.

They identified 73 resource-related measures across six categories:

Transport,
Fuel,
Heating,
Electricity,
Water,
Waste

But the important finding for your project is that there is a gap between what research recommends and what construction companies actually implement. Only 26% of the reviewed studies measured resource consumption directly on construction sites. The authors therefore recommend better on-site monitoring and real-time evaluation for informed decision-making.

# Finding

Construction projects consume multiple resources such as labour, materials, equipment, energy, water and transportation. Although numerous resource-management strategies have been proposed in research, their practical implementation at construction sites remains challenging. Existing approaches also have limitations in real-time monitoring and decision-making. Therefore, there is a need for a computational system that can organize project activities, track resource requirements and availability, identify conflicts, and support timely resource-planning decisions.

The study finds that poor planning, inefficient resource allocation, and inadequate risk management are strongly associated with schedule overruns. It specifically highlights labour, materials and equipment allocation as important for maintaining workflow continuity.

# 4. Labour multiplier driven approach for budgeting project resources.(Suet-Wai Tracy Cheung, Ming-Fung Francis Siu, Ping-Chuen Albert Chan-2024)

📄 Read Full Paper
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666165924001777?

The paper points out that construction manpower demand is often budgeted based on planners' personal experience. It proposes an analytical method for estimating labour demand throughout different stages of a project, using data from 91 completed public construction projects across 21 project types.

Labour demand estimation → Resource availability → Resource conflict → Activity prioritization → DSA-based scheduling.





# 5.Tracing resource flows and reducing environmental impacts during construction: Assessment framework for on-site waste efficiency.(Lea Hasselsteen, Anders Damgaard, Aleksander P. Otovic, Harpa Birgisdóttir, Kai Kanafani-2025)

📄 Read Full Paper
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652625015938?

The study examines material/resource flows during construction and identifies inefficiencies in how materials are ordered, handled, used, and wasted. In its case study, about 9.1% of delivered materials were wasted, with over-ordering and improper planning identified as important causes.

Material requirement → Material availability → Allocation → Usage → Remaining/Wasted material
